.. _render-3d:

3D View
=======

The 3D view allows the user to render 3D localization data. Open it
with ``View > 3D view`` (:kbd:`Ctrl+Shift+R`):

- With a single pick region selected (``Tools > Pick``) it shows that pick.
- Without a pick (or with several) it shows the current field of view of the
  main window, rotated about its center - so any region can be looked at in 3D
  by zooming to it and pressing the shortcut.
- Pressed again with nothing changed, it only brings the window to the front,
  keeping the rotation; a new pick or a new field of view reloads it.

Some of the display settings (blur method, colormap, etc.) are taken
over from the main window, see :ref:`render-3d-display-settings`.

The arrow keys move the shown region. In the pick mode, they move the pick
itself, in the main window too.

The user may perform multiple actions in the 3D view, including:
saving rotated localizations, building animations (.mp4 format), rotating by a
specified angle, see below.

Rendering in the 3D view runs in the background, as in the main
window:

- Rotating and panning never block the interface, and a burst of mouse
  movements renders only the newest orientation.
- Fields of view with many localizations are previewed as a subset while dragging
  (``interaction_subsample``, see :ref:`render-cpu-usage`) and sharpened as
  soon as the drag pauses.
- The renders use the GPU when it is enabled (see :ref:`render-gpu-rendering`).

When rotating by a specified angle, the dialog offers a ``Rotate around``
choice between **Localizations** (the default) and **World**.
``Localizations`` rotates around the data's own axes - the axes shown by the
axes icon, which rotate together with the data - so each entered angle changes
the corresponding displayed angle by exactly that amount. ``World`` rotates
around the fixed screen/camera axes instead.

.. _render-3d-navigation:

Navigating the 3D window
------------------------

The zoom and pan controls match the main window's, see
:ref:`render-navigation`.

- **Rotate**: with the Rotate tool (``Tools > Rotate``, :kbd:`Ctrl+R`, active
  when the window opens), drag with the left mouse button (trackball). Hold
  :kbd:`S` while dragging to rotate in steps of 15 degrees. The other tool,
  ``Tools > Measure`` (:kbd:`Ctrl+M`), measures distances instead.
- **Zoom**:

  - :kbd:`Ctrl` (:kbd:`Cmd` on macOS) + the mouse wheel (or trackpad scroll; a
    pinch works too) zooms about the cursor.
  - Drag a rectangle with :kbd:`Shift` + the left button to zoom to it (as in
    the main window, the rectangle stretches towards the bottom right and
    releasing above or left of the start cancels).
  - :kbd:`Ctrl` +/- zoom about the center.

- **Pan**: drag with the right or the middle mouse button, or with :kbd:`Alt`
  (:kbd:`Option` on macOS) + the left button. This moves the view over the
  loaded localizations. With the Measure tool pan with the middle
  button or :kbd:`Alt` + the left button there.
- **Arrow keys**: unlike panning, they move the shown region itself - the pick
  (also in the main window) or the field of view - and load the localizations
  of the moved region.
- **Reset**: triple click fits the loaded region into the window (:kbd:`Home`
  does the same); :kbd:`Shift` + triple click also resets the rotation.
  :kbd:`1`, :kbd:`2` and :kbd:`3` select the XY, XZ and YZ projections.

Rotations turn the data about a point at the center of the view. Its depth
follows the data: after every pan or zoom, it is set to the median depth of
the localizations visible in the view, so zooming in and panning to a
structure lets you rotate around it. Further rotation controls, all with the
Rotate tool:

- Holding :kbd:`Ctrl`/:kbd:`Cmd` makes a vertical drag spin the data around
  the screen z-axis (in the screen plane) instead of tilting it; a horizontal
  drag still turns it around the screen y-axis. To rotate around z only, lock
  the z-axis (see below).
- The rotation axis can be frozen by pressing :kbd:`X`/:kbd:`Y`/:kbd:`Z` to
  freeze around the corresponding axis. By default the frozen rotation is
  around the data's own axes (Localizations frame); holding
  :kbd:`Ctrl`/:kbd:`Cmd` together with :kbd:`X`/:kbd:`Y`/:kbd:`Z` instead
  rotates around the fixed screen/World axes.
- The z-axis can now be frozen by pressing :kbd:`Z` alone (vertical dragging
  spins around it); :kbd:`Ctrl`/:kbd:`Cmd` is only needed for the z-axis if
  you want to rotate it in the World frame, or to spin around the screen
  z-axis when no axis is frozen.

.. _render-3d-menus:

Menus
-----

The 3D view has its own ``File``, ``View`` and ``Tools`` menus.

.. _render-3d-file-menu:

File
~~~~

``Save rotated localizations...`` (:kbd:`Ctrl+S`)
   Saves the localizations shown, of one channel or all of them, together
   with the rotation (angles and quaternion) and the pick or field of view in
   the metadata. Open them rotated again with ``File > Open rotated
   localizations`` in the main window, see :ref:`render-open-rotated`.

``Export current view...`` (:kbd:`Ctrl+E`)
   Saves the rendered image as ``.png`` or ``.tif``. The file name carries the
   rotation angles, and a ``.yaml`` file next to it records the rotation, the
   field of view, the display pixel size and the contrast. Without a scale bar
   shown, a second image with a scale bar is saved as ``_scalebar.png``.

``Build an animation...`` (:kbd:`Ctrl+Shift+E`)
   See :ref:`render-animation` below.

``Help``
   Opens this page.

.. _render-3d-view-menu:

View
~~~~

``Display settings...`` (:kbd:`Ctrl+D`)
   Opens the display settings of the 3D view, see
   :ref:`render-3d-display-settings` below.

``Show/hide legend`` (:kbd:`Ctrl+L`), ``Show/hide rotation`` (:kbd:`Ctrl+P`), ``Show/hide rotation angles``
   Toggle the overlays: the channel legend of multichannel data, the axes icon
   that shows the current orientation, and the rotation angles in degrees.

``Rotate by angle...`` (:kbd:`Ctrl+Shift+R`)
   Rotates by angles typed in, around the data's own axes or the fixed screen
   axes (see ``Rotate around`` above).

``Reset rotation`` (:kbd:`Ctrl+Shift+W`)
   Returns to the unrotated view.

``Fit image to window`` (:kbd:`Ctrl+W`)
   Fits the loaded region into the window, like :kbd:`Home`.

``XY projection``, ``XZ projection``, ``YZ projection``
   Show the data along one of the three axes, like :kbd:`1`, :kbd:`2` and
   :kbd:`3`.

.. _render-3d-tools-menu:

Tools
~~~~~

``Rotate`` (:kbd:`Ctrl+R`)
   Left dragging rotates the data, see :ref:`render-3d-navigation`. Active when
   the window opens.

``Measure`` (:kbd:`Ctrl+M`)
   Measures distances as in the main window (see :ref:`render-measure`): a left
   click adds a point, a right click finishes the current measurement or, clicked
   again, deletes the last one. The distances are measured in the screen plane,
   i.e., in the current projection of the data.

.. _render-3d-display-settings:

Display settings
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The display settings of the 3D view are separate from the main window's, so
the 3D view can be tuned without changing the main image. When the 3D view is
opened or reloaded, the blur method, the colormap and the settings of the
adaptive histogram and of the jittered triangulation are taken over from the
main window; everything else is set here:

- the display pixel size (with ``dynamic``) and the contrast (``Min. density``,
  ``Max. density``) and colormap;
- the blur method, with ``Min. Blur (nm)``, the adaptive histogram's
  ``Leaf capacity`` and the jittered triangulation's ``Passes``, ``Jitter``
  and ``Max. localizations`` (see :ref:`render-display-settings`);
- the scale bar: its length, ``Automatic length`` and ``Print scale bar
  length``.

.. _render-animation:

Build an animation
------------------

.. rst-class:: shortcut

Shortcut: :kbd:`Ctrl+Shift+E`

Build an animation with ``File > Build an animation...`` in the 3D view:

1. Rotate, zoom and pan to each view the video should show and click
   ``Add this position`` (``Stay in the position`` adds a pause,
   ``Remove last position`` removes the last one). Each listed position has a
   ``Show position`` button that returns the view to it.
2. Set the durations of the transitions and the frame rate (``FPS``, 30 by
   default).
3. Click ``Build animation``.

The frames are rendered in the background, at the resolution set in the
animation dialog (``Resolution (px)``, by default the window's size), so the
windows stay usable while the video is built.

While the video is built, a progress dialog shows how many frames have been
rendered. ``Cancel`` stops the build and deletes the unfinished video file.

``Transition`` sets how the motion is timed between the positions:

*Stop at each position* (default)
   Accelerates and decelerates between every two positions, coming to rest at
   each of them.
*Smooth*
   Starts and ends at rest and passes through the positions without abrupt
   changes of direction or speed (it still comes to rest where the motion
   reverses, stops or turns by 90 degrees or more).
*Constant speed*
   Moves at a constant speed between every two positions, with sharp turns at
   the positions.

``Rotation speed (deg/s)`` is only used to suggest a duration when a position
is added: the angle turned since the previous position divided by the speed.
The suggested durations can be edited, and every transition style takes
exactly the durations listed; the style only changes how the speed varies
within each of them, not how long they take.
